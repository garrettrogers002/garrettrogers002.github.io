Poetry of William Blake

A Divine Image

            Cruelty has a human heart,
            And Jealousy a human face;
          Terror the human form divine,
            And Secresy the human dress.
          
          The human dress is forged iron,
            The human form a fiery forge,
          The human face a furnace sealed,
            The human heart its hungry gorge.

AI Analysis

This poem explores the darker aspects of human nature, depicting the destructive emotions of cruelty, jealousy, and terror as distinctly human qualities. The poem's imagery is striking and unsettling, with human body parts used to represent the destructive forces. The repetition of "human" emphasizes the extent to which these negative traits are inherent to humanity.

Compared to Blake's other works, this poem is less overtly religious and more focused on the darker aspects of human existence. However, it shares the same themes of innocence and experience, as it depicts the struggle between good and evil within the human soul.

In the context of the time period, this poem reflects the Romantic movement's fascination with the exploration of the human psyche and the expression of strong emotions. It also aligns with the Romantic emphasis on symbolism and the use of nature to represent human qualities.
